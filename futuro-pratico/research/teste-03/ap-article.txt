Share Share Facebook Copy Link copied Print Email X LinkedIn Bluesky Flipboard Pinterest Reddit CHICAGO (AP) — Tesla has unveiled its new Roadster sports car, which can thrust itself in the air, among other engineering bells and whistles. But drivers shouldn’t expect to take it for a spin anytime soon. Elon Musk’s electric car company rolled out its second-generation Roadster at a Thursday event, showing off a set of cold gas thrusters and a “multi-mode” suction system that can fling the car in the air for short spurts and spin the vehicle mid-flight. “Turns out the future does have flying cars,” Musk, Tesla’s CEO, wrote on his social media platform X shortly after. That future could be a ways off, however, given the regulatory hurdles for flying vehicles and the tangle of state rules that still need to be met. “I’m not sure every state is going to allow a driver in the middle of traffic to suddenly elevate himself and fly,” said David Vladeck, a Georgetown Law professor and former director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection. “There is going to be a long process to let anything like that on a road.” Thursday’s event was hosted at the Texas rocket testing and development center for SpaceX — Musk’s publicly-traded rocket company — and Tesla’s executives noted that they “borrowed” some SpaceX technology to get the new Roadster in the air. Beyond quick jumps and flips, Tesla also said the car can accelerate from zero to 60 miles (96.6 km) an hour in less than a second. This week’s launch arrived after years of delays and anticipation around the new Roadster — and Tesla shares climbed in morning trading on Friday before falling amid a sell-off in companies tied to artificial intelligence. The wait isn’t completely over. Musk said production for consumers won’t begin until 2028. Tesla also didn’t immediately set a price tag on its latest EV. Bryant Walker Smith, former vice chair of a Department of Transportation advisory group on innovation, was unimpressed — though he did note Musk’s ability to keep Tesla’s stock high. “This was a demonstration of a one-off car using a technology around for many years and will not be coming to public roads in 2028,” said Smith, a law professor at the University of South Carolina. “Tesla pretty uniquely has been able to sell itself on a future that is an order of magnitude greater than reality.” Read More Musk has repeatedly said that the future of driving will be autonomous — and Austin-based Tesla has focused much of its business on building out self-driving features for its vehicles and, more recently, launching its own (still limited) robotaxi business . But the company maintains that the Roadster will be made to have humans behind the wheel, calling the EV the “best of the last human-driven cars.” Thursday’s demo was technically autonomous “to make sure we don’t kill anyone,” Musk noted. But long-term, he said, the new Roadster will be “the only manually driven car that Tesla makes.” “This is not necessarily a safety-optimized car,” Musk later added, met by laughter from the audience. But “it takes the fun meter to 11 out of 10.” Tesla’s first Roadster was launched back in 2008. And in 2018, Musk went as far as placing one on a rocket — with a mannequin dubbed “Starman” in the driver’s seat — and launching it into space . Again, the second-generation Roadster isn’t immediately hitting the market. And whether the ambitious new vehicle boosts the company’s bottom line remains to be seen.
